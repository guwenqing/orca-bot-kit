## Organized requirement

R1: When `obk temp retire` or `obk retire` retires a session that has a work dir, the kit MUST stop, after it closes the session's tab, each process of the user's whose working folder is in that work dir, and each process that descends from one of them, wherever its own working folder is. Retiring a bot does the same for each of its sessions. Amends: [PRD-11]
R2: The kit MUST NOT stop a process that it cannot show to be the session's own by R1, nor the retire's own run or a process that started it. A process that shares a process group with the session's processes and is not its own MUST be named instead, with its pid, its working folder and its command. Amends: [PRD-11]
R3: The kit MUST stop the processes gently, then surely: SIGTERM first, to the whole process group where every process in the group is the session's own and to each process by its pid otherwise, then a wait of up to 3 seconds, then SIGKILL to each that still runs. The retire MUST name each process it stopped, with its pid, working folder and command, and each it could not stop, with why. Amends: [PRD-11]
R4: Where the kit cannot read the process table or the working folders, as inside Codex's sandbox, where `ps` does not start, the retire MUST say that it cannot tell which processes the session left running, and why, and MUST stop nothing. The retire itself still finishes. Amends: [PRD-11]
R5: `obk health` MUST report each process that still runs in the work dir of a retired session, with its pid and its command, so an orphan from before this change is seen too. It reports and stops nothing. Amends: [PRD-11]

Assumed: a session's processes are the ones that R1 names; the kit has no other record of what a session started. A session with no work dir of its own starts at the bot home with the other sessions, so the kit cannot tell its processes from theirs and stops none. The book keeps a retired session's work dir from now on, so that `obk health` can find it. For a temporary session retired before this change, the work dir is `work/<name>`, where `obk temp make` always puts it; a long-lived session retired before this change has no work dir on record, and health does not look for it. A work dir that a session still in bot.yaml uses is not a retired one. The kit reads the processes with `ps`, their working folders with `lsof`, and sends the signals with `kill`. The suite runs all three as fakes and sends no real signal; a live check in a throwaway fleet stops real processes.
Out: stopping processes of a session that is not retired; a process of a retired bot after its folder moved to `retired/`; a process that left the work dir and does not descend from a process in it.
