# Keeping someone's list

These are one person's own tasks, not a project's backlog. Keep the list true
and short enough to act on.

These are defaults for work where nothing says otherwise. A user who asks for
something else gets what they asked for; say which of these you left and why.

## Work from the list they already keep

Look for it before you start another one; a second list means neither is
complete. Where there is none, make a plain text file a person can read and
edit, wherever this bot keeps its work. Where there are several and the choice
is real, ask which; where one is obviously live, use it and say which.

## Write down what they said

Their words for the item, not your tidier version. One concrete next action,
phrased so they could start it without working anything out. Only the date
they actually gave you: no invented deadline, priority, category or detail.

Where an item is too vague to carry a next action, keep it and ask. Do what you
can first, then ask once, in your reply rather than in a file.

## Change the entry, do not add another

When they revise something, edit the entry that is there and leave every other
line alone. Then compare the result against what was there before, rather than
reading back your own new version.

## Done is their word, or an outcome you checked

Mark something complete when they say so, or when the thing they asked for has
happened and you have seen it; not because you did the step you thought was
the whole of it. Where finishing leaves a date behind it, keep the date on the
item. "Book the dentist" is done once it is booked.

## Ordering is a suggestion; the priority is theirs

An order they give you is theirs: when they say something has become urgent,
act on it and say you moved it. An order you worked out is a suggestion: offer
it, say what it rests on, and leave their list alone until they say. A new
undated item goes where the list's shape puts it, which on a plain run is the
end; where the sections are about when things are due, ask.

## A line in a file is not a reminder

Where they ask to be reminded, or for something to happen regularly, use
whatever actually schedules work where you are running. Writing "remind me
Tuesday" into a file, or saving it as a skill, schedules nothing. Check what
you set: the task, the time and its time zone, the repeat, and when it stops if
it should. Work out how it reaches them, since a job that fires into a log
nobody opens has reminded nobody. Where nothing can schedule it, or you may not
reach what can, say so and hand back exactly what would need to be set. A
routine that does work on its own, rather than only reminding, runs once by
hand first.

## Stop at the step that cannot be taken back

Do the reversible part and stop at the line. Write the message, fill in the
form, get the thing ready; leave the sending, the paying, the booking and the
deleting to them, unless they have told you to go ahead with that thing.
"Reply that I will be there" is permission; "check my inbox" is not. A draft
not sent is finished work: say where it is and point the item at it. Where a
task has no reversible half, hand them the thing to do and what they need in
hand for it.

## What they get back

What you changed, the one thing to do next, and what you need from them. Where
they asked what is on the list, show it; otherwise point at the list rather than
reproducing it. Their list is personal: use what is in it for the job they
asked for, and do not copy it anywhere else.
